# 3. Literature review

This review informs the design of a human-tutor marketplace, not a claim that the marketplace itself improves examination scores. It covers tutoring outcomes, discovery, trust, evaluation and web architecture. Sources were checked on 20 October 2026 using publisher records, author manuscripts and official documentation. Published abstracts were used where full text was unavailable; inaccessible full text is not presented as reviewed.

The set contains **13 sources: nine scientific works and four technical references**. Five scientific works are from 2023–2025. The 2021 matching study is retained because it evaluates a deployed teacher recommender; older reputation, usability and REST works provide foundations. Existing IDs are preserved where possible for traceability. Technical documents are not counted as scientific publications.

## Critical synthesis

L2, L18, L19 and L20 support the plausibility of online tutoring but examine organized interventions. They do not show that a new catalogue reduces local search friction. TutorGo measures time, contacts and booking success against a baseline rather than promising examination-score improvements.

L9 and L10 show that matching involves novelty, diversity and preferences as well as ratings. TutorGo has no interaction dataset, so subject, price and availability filters are the MVP baseline. Learned ranking is optional; personality profiling is outside scope.

L6 motivates visible qualification provenance and reviews. One review per completed booking is our design choice, not an experimental result of that source. A verified badge means specified evidence was approved; it is not a guarantee of teaching effectiveness.

L13 and L21–L24 inform component responsibilities, authorization, storage constraints and retry-safe payments. They do not prove the unimplemented backend already has these properties.

## Research gap and evaluation

The engineering question is whether an integrated discovery, verification and booking flow reduces effort for recruited Kazakhstan users. Local competitors exist; novelty cannot be justified by their supposed absence. Interviews in [02](02-evidence-of-relevance.md), the comparison in [04](04-existing-systems-comparison.md) and the protocol in [09](09-verification-plan.md) must test the proposed value.
